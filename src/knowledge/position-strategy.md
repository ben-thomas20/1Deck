# Position Strategy in Texas Hold'em

Position is one of the most important concepts in poker. Your position relative to the dealer button determines when you act during each betting round, and acting later gives you a significant advantage because you get to see what your opponents do first.

## The Positions

At a full ring table (8-9 players), positions are named clockwise:

### Early Position (EP)
- **Under the Gun (UTG)**: First to act preflop (left of the big blind). The worst position. You need a strong hand to play from here because everyone acts after you.
- **UTG+1**: Second-worst position.

### Middle Position (MP)
- A moderate position. You can play a wider range of hands than early position but should still be selective.

### Late Position (LP)
- **Cutoff (CO)**: Second-to-last to act. A strong position — you can play a wide range.
- **Button (BTN)**: The best position at the table. You act last on every post-flop street. You can play the widest range of hands.

### The Blinds
- **Small Blind (SB)**: Posts half the big blind. Acts second-to-last preflop but first post-flop. A bad position because you're forced to put money in and you act first on later streets.
- **Big Blind (BB)**: Posts the full blind. Gets to act last preflop and can "check their option" if nobody raised. Post-flop, acts second (right after the small blind).

## Why Position Matters

### Information Advantage
When you act last, you see what every other player does before you make your decision. Did they check (showing weakness)? Did they bet (showing strength)? This information is incredibly valuable.

### Pot Control
In position, you can choose to bet for value or check behind to control the pot size. Out of position, you must act without knowing what your opponent will do.

### Bluffing Opportunities
Bluffs are more effective in late position. If everyone checks to you, you can bet and often win the pot regardless of your hand.

## Starting Hand Recommendations by Position

### Early Position (play tight)
Play only premium hands: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, AKs, AKo, AQs
Approximately top 8-10% of hands.

### Middle Position (slightly wider)
Add: TT, 99, AJs, AQo, KQs
Approximately top 12-15% of hands.

### Late Position / Button (play wide)
Add: 88-22, suited connectors (87s, 76s), suited aces (A5s, A4s), broadways (KJo, QJo)
Approximately top 25-35% of hands.

### Blinds
From the small blind, play tight unless you're completing against limpers. From the big blind, you already have money invested — defend against reasonable raises with a wider range.

## Heads-Up Position Rules
In heads-up play (2 players), position changes:
- The dealer/button posts the small blind and acts first preflop.
- Post-flop, the dealer/button acts last (the positional advantage).
- This means the button has a disadvantage preflop but an advantage on all subsequent streets.
