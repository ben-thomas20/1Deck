# Pot Odds and Implied Odds

Understanding pot odds is essential for making profitable decisions in poker. Pot odds tell you whether a call is mathematically justified based on the size of the pot relative to the cost of calling.

## What Are Pot Odds?

Pot odds are the ratio of the current pot size to the amount you must call.

**Formula**: Pot Odds = Pot Size / Bet to Call

**Example**: The pot is $100 and your opponent bets $50. The pot is now $150, and you need to call $50.
Pot Odds = $150 / $50 = 3:1 (or 25%)

This means you need to win more than 25% of the time for a call to be profitable.

## Counting Outs

"Outs" are the cards remaining in the deck that will improve your hand to a likely winner.

### Common Draw Outs
- **Flush draw** (4 cards to a flush): 9 outs
- **Open-ended straight draw** (e.g., 8-7 on a 9-6 board): 8 outs
- **Gutshot straight draw** (need one specific rank): 4 outs
- **Two overcards** (e.g., AK on a low board): 6 outs
- **Flush draw + straight draw**: 15 outs (a very strong draw)
- **Set to full house/quads**: 7 outs

## The Rule of 2 and 4

A quick way to estimate your chance of hitting an out:
- **Flop to river** (2 cards to come): Multiply outs × 4
- **Turn to river** (1 card to come): Multiply outs × 2

**Example**: You have a flush draw (9 outs) on the flop.
- Chance of hitting by the river: 9 × 4 = 36%
- Chance of hitting on the turn only: 9 × 2 = 18%

## Applying Pot Odds to Decisions

**Step 1**: Calculate your pot odds (what percentage of the time you need to win).
**Step 2**: Estimate your equity (your chance of winning, based on outs).
**Step 3**: If your equity > pot odds needed, calling is profitable.

**Example**:
- Pot is $200, opponent bets $100, pot becomes $300, you must call $100.
- Pot odds: $300 / $100 = 3:1, meaning you need > 25% equity.
- You have a flush draw (9 outs ≈ 36% with 2 cards to come).
- 36% > 25% → **Call is profitable**.

## Implied Odds

Implied odds account for additional money you expect to win on future streets if you hit your draw.

Even if pot odds alone don't justify a call, implied odds might — if you expect your opponent to pay you off when you complete your draw.

**Good implied odds situations**:
- You have a hidden draw (set mining with a pocket pair)
- Your opponent has a big stack and is aggressive
- Your draw will make a very strong hand (nut flush, not just any flush)

**Bad implied odds situations**:
- The draw is obvious (4 to a flush on the board)
- Your opponent is tight and will fold when the draw completes
- You're drawing to a non-nut hand (e.g., low flush could lose to higher flush)

## Reverse Implied Odds

Sometimes completing your draw can cost you money if your opponent has a better hand. Example: You make a flush with 8♣ 7♣, but your opponent has A♣ K♣ for a higher flush. This is a "reverse implied odds" situation.

Be cautious with:
- Non-nut flush draws
- Low straight draws when higher straights are possible
- Bottom set when the board is coordinated
