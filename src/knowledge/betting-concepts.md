# Betting Concepts in Texas Hold'em

## Types of Bets

### Value Bet
A bet made when you believe you have the best hand and want your opponent to call with a worse hand.

**Example**: You have A♠ K♠ on a board of A♥ 7♦ 3♣. Your top pair with the best kicker is very likely the best hand. You bet for value, hoping a worse ace (AJ, AT) or a pair of sevens calls.

**Key principle**: Bet an amount your opponent will call. Betting too large may fold out the hands you want to call. Betting too small doesn't extract enough value.

### Bluff
A bet made when you have a weak hand, trying to make your opponent fold a better hand.

**Good bluff situations**:
- You're in position and your opponent has shown weakness (checking)
- The board favors your perceived range (e.g., an ace hits the board and you raised preflop)
- Your opponent is capable of folding

**Bad bluff situations**:
- Multiple opponents in the pot (harder to bluff several players)
- Your opponent is a "calling station" (never folds)
- There's no logical story for why you would have a strong hand

### Semi-Bluff
A bet with a hand that's not currently the best but has good potential to improve (a draw).

**Example**: You have 8♦ 7♦ on a board of 9♦ 6♦ 2♠. You have a flush draw and an open-ended straight draw. If your opponent folds, you win immediately. If they call, you still have many outs to make the best hand.

Semi-bluffs are generally better than pure bluffs because you have equity even when called.

### Continuation Bet (C-Bet)
A bet on the flop by the preflop raiser, regardless of whether the flop helped their hand.

**Why it works**: The preflop raiser has a perceived range advantage. They could have big pairs or overcards. Since the flop misses most hands, a C-bet folds out hands that didn't connect.

**Typical C-bet size**: 50-75% of the pot.

**When to skip the C-bet**:
- The flop is very wet (many draws possible) and you have nothing
- Multiple opponents called preflop
- The flop heavily favors the caller's range (e.g., 8-7-6 when you raised with AK)

### Check-Raise
Checking to your opponent, then raising when they bet. A powerful play that represents extreme strength.

**Used for**:
- **Value**: You have a very strong hand and want to build a big pot
- **Bluff**: Representing a strong hand to fold out medium-strength hands
- **Trapping**: Luring an aggressive player into betting so you can raise

## Bet Sizing

### Preflop
- **Standard open raise**: 2.5x to 3x the big blind
- **Add 1 big blind** for each limper already in the pot

### Postflop
- **Small bet (25-33% pot)**: Thin value, probing, cheap bluff
- **Medium bet (50-75% pot)**: Standard value bet or semi-bluff
- **Large bet (pot or more)**: Polarized (either very strong or a bluff)

### General Principles
- Bet larger on wet boards (many draws) to charge draws
- Bet smaller on dry boards (few draws) since there's less to protect against
- Your bluffs and value bets should use similar sizing so opponents can't distinguish them

## Common Beginner Mistakes with Betting
1. **Betting too small** with strong hands (letting draws in cheaply)
2. **Only betting with good hands** (becoming too predictable)
3. **Calling too much** instead of raising or folding (the "calling station" trap)
4. **Bluffing too frequently** against opponents who never fold
5. **Not sizing bets relative to the pot** (making random-sized bets)
